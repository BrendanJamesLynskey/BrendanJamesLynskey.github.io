For Optalysys

Light, encryption and simulation

Optalysys works on optical computing, including for fully homomorphic encryption. These are the sites, decks and simulators I built to learn the areas that work touches.

I built all of this for my own learning, and I am still working my way through it. Each page below says what it does and why I made it. They follow one path: the hardware, the workloads it might run, and the engineering that makes a simulator's answers trustworthy.

The hardware

How light computes, from the device physics up to a whole module, modelled in enough detail to ask whether it pays.

Interactive site · 17 chapters, simulator in the page

Inside a Photonic Link Module

What it doesA simulator of a photonic link module (arXiv:2608.21536), explained chapter by chapter: the modulators, the optical Fourier transform, homodyne detection, calibration, and how a 4-point kernel becomes an NTT inside a real FHE bootstrap. A TypeScript port of the simulator runs in the page, checked against the Python reference. Part II models a future chip with the photonics stacked on its logic.

Why I built itTo understand the hardware properly by modelling it, layer by layer, and to see with numbers where it pays and where it falls short.

Silicon Photonics Fundamentals

What it doesThe device physics under every optical link: how silicon guides, splits and filters light; how modulators and germanium photodiodes put data on it and take it off; and where the laser, the package and the picojoules per bit come from.

Why I built itMy background is electronics. This is the optics I needed underneath everything else.

The workloads

What the optics might be asked to do: encrypted computing, and moving and transforming data for AI.

Deck series and simulator · 5 decks, tested code

FHE Accelerator Simulators

What it doesCKKS as a hardware workload, the anatomy of bootstrapping, a SimPy simulator of an FHE accelerator (live in the browser), optical NTT engines, and the design space: is a design bound by NTT throughput or by key traffic, and does an optical NTT engine win once conversion energy and precision are counted?

Why I built itTo learn FHE from the hardware side: where an accelerator spends its time and energy, and where optics could help.

Deck series and results site · 5 decks, an experiment

Compute in Transit

What it doesWhich data movements in LLM training and serving a photonic link module could compute on as the data crosses the link, how much work each needs per byte, and an experiment on the strongest candidate: a fixed frequency band in place of DeMo's top-k for compressing gradients. At this scale the answer is no, and the results site shows why.

Why I built itTo find out whether the module could help AI training, by experiment rather than by argument.

Fourier Optics for Inference

What it doesCould a Fourier-optical engine accelerate LLM inference? The physics with its precision and energy bill, where transforms appear in inference workloads, and a disaggregated-serving simulator with an optical transform device in the prefill pool, live in the browser.

Why I built itTo put numbers on where optical transforms could matter for serving AI models.
